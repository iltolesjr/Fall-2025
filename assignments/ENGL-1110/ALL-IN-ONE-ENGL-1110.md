## Final Reflection — My Voice

I’m analytical. I think in variables and operators, like the word "and" joining ideas. Early on I joked about English being subjective. Now I see I can be subjective and analytical at the same time—read the chapter, speak my thoughts, then shape them into something precise.

AI wasn’t there to fake it; it was there to help me iterate. We read what was assigned, then I talked it through. Sometimes we made bad versions, even submitted the wrong one. That’s part of learning—version control for writing.

Cisneros and Children of the Sea made me feel the heavy presence without naming it. I know that thing. I lived it. Being Black in systems that mark you: courts, foster care, landlords. I don’t remember the jokes adults made; I remember the moments hiding, the missing mother, the cycles that repeat unless someone teaches you the before.

I haven’t seen my daughters in years now. That absence is a constant, and it shapes how I read stories about separation and return. When a chapter shows a family pulled apart by policy or force, I don’t need a quote to understand it—I carry the evidence in my own life. This portfolio is part of how I keep moving forward: honoring what I’ve lived, citing what I’ve read, and refusing to let the systems that divide us also silence us.

Words are variables. America is stuck in a for-loop of bullshit: racism, policies that murder quietly, databases that decide futures. My genealogy shows the pattern—draft cards, enlistments, World Wars—men taken, families split, then judged for how broken they look.

Thomas’s chapter by chapter, or Danticat’s pages, are more than quotes; they’re anchors. When the story spends time on the wrong man’s whimsy while kids should be hiding, I feel that mismatch. Whoever wrote that likely never got taken from their mother and ran from foster care to get back home. I did.

Love isn’t a feeling only. It’s doing the things that protect the people you say you love—like voting, like refusing to sell hate in new flavors. Every time certain people take office, the hard R flies more; donations reward cruelty; guns go up for auction; migrants get bused to make points. Then budgets explode to undo unlawful moves that voters could have prevented locally.

I’m forty, still never got the IT job I planned since 1999. I study because it’s a goal and because finishing matters, even if the market dips. But I’m also a father fighting courts to see my daughters. I feel like the native kids being taken away. I see how policies split Black women from spouses, crash economies, then criminalize, block voting, block work, block school with cost. The cycle is the product. Rich people learned they can sell the opposite of good and profit from it.

If America wants me to fight for it, stop dividing tight-knit families the census used to show under one roof. Stop telling me we’re the best while we’re best at breaking what’s ours. If you bought slaves and built a majority that way, then admit you needed us. We didn’t need you. We toiled Mississippi and Arkansas for centuries. You struggle to toil 60 years.

I’m not asking for a king; I’m asking for honest measures and a way out of the loop. Teach kids the before so the after isn’t just someone else’s narrative with weaponized words. In this class, I learned to use the operator "and" to stitch claims to evidence and lived math to meaning—so the page numbers aren’t just citations; they’re coordinates for truth.
